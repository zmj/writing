recvmail.net

October 26, 2026

I built recvmail.net earlier this month. It sells ephemeral, receive-only email inboxes for a cent. It’s a bet on how personal agents will fit into e-commerce generally, and specifically a guess at what they might need to buy.

My core thesis is that agents will be reliable enough to autonomously complete consequential tasks, like spending real money. But trust will lag success rates - see Waymo, where being vastly safer than a human driver isn’t enough for many people. So agents will by default have isolated, bounded, environments and resources, not full delegation of their human’s.

In payments, I think that looks like the equivalent of a prepaid debit card given to an employee (or a teenager). Something that allows the agent to spend real money, but structurally capped; not a credit card or bank account. The market seems to be moving towards stablecoin payments from both ends: micropayments to content providers in place of advertising, and friendly regulation making it easier for consumers to hold a stablecoin balance. I don’t have strong opinions on specific coins or rails, but I’m betting something in this space becomes ubiquitous over the next two years.

Email will be a similar story. Agents may or may not be commonly allowed to read their human’s mail, but I think it will be rare to fully delegate that identity. Acting as a human’s email identity - sending mail or signing up - will usually have the human in the loop. That means email verification will remain a meaningful signal for website operators, which leaves a gap to be filled by a product like recvmail.

An account created with a recvmail address isn’t human. But I think email verification gates are often intended to filter for “authority and ability to spend money” - and recvmail addresses are pre-qualified. They’re meaningfully different than bots or scrapers that consume resources without the possibility of return.

A final lingering question: if useful parts of the internet become gated behind crypto micropayments, won’t that incentivize agents to do crimes to acquire crypto? Probably. But since when have plausible worries about second-order consequences slowed down anything AI-related?
